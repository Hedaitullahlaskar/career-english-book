## Decision A — Linen contract

**The conflict.** Level 4 Module 1 ends with the Bengal Textile Supplies linen contract signed for **two years at a fixed rate** (written confirmation titled "2027–2028"). Later passages describe the same contract coming up for **renewal one year later**, as if it were an annual contract.

| Location | PDF page | Current wording | Implied contract period | Conflict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L4 Lesson 1.1 (Opening) | 617 | "Bengal Textile Supplies, is raising prices for the new contract year." | Annual, before the negotiation | None. This is the starting position. |
| L4 Lesson 1.3 (dialogue) | 629 | "We could come down from 12% to 8%, if the hotel is willing to sign for a longer term — say, two years." / Arif asks for "a fixed rate for the full two years" | Moving to two years | None. This is where the term changes. |
| L4 Lesson 1.5 (close) | 640 | "we've agreed on an 8% increase on last year's per-unit rate, fixed for a two-year term, with a review limited to delivery costs only after twelve months — no review of the core rate." | **Two years, rate fixed** | **Canonical in Module 1.** |
| L4 Lesson 1.5 (written confirmation) | 641 | "Subject: Confirming Linen Supply Contract Terms — 2027–2028 … fixed for a two-year term." | **Two years (2027–2028)** | **Canonical in Module 1.** |
| L4 Lesson 1.5 (industry overlay) | 642 | "a two-year linen supply contract closes with every term restated aloud" | Two years | Consistent with 1.5. |
| L4 Lesson 8.2 (industry overlay) | 819 | "Mr. Mostafa from Bengal Textile Supplies, the same vendor from Module 1's linen negotiation, returns a year later for the contract's renewal: 'As our relationship has grown this past year, it's worth revisiting the delivery terms as well as the rate.'" | Annual (renewal after one year, rate revisited) | **Conflicts with 1.5.** The rate was fixed for two years; only delivery costs were reviewable after twelve months. |
| L4 Capstone, Step 1 (setting) | 848 | "Bengal Textile Supplies' Mr. Tariq Mostafa calls about the annual renewal of the linen supply contract Arif first negotiated a year ago in Module 1 … the tone is noticeably firmer than last year's call." | **Annual** | **Conflicts with 1.5** ("annual renewal" one year into a two-year fixed contract). |
| L4 Capstone, Step 1 (dialogue) | 848 | Mr. Mostafa: "regionally we're seeing most suppliers move toward a 15% adjustment." / Arif: "given a year of steady, growing volume with you, we were expecting something closer to a 5 to 6% adjustment." | Annual (rate renegotiated after one year) | **Conflicts with 1.5** (the core rate was fixed and not reviewable). |
| L4 Capstone, Step 1 (dialogue) | 848 | Arif: "if we commit to a two-year term instead of renewing year to year, would that let you come down closer to 7%?" | **Year-to-year**, with two years offered as something new | **Conflicts with 1.5** (the hotel already has a two-year term). |
| L4 Capstone, Step 1 (dialogue) | 849 | Arif: "we've agreed on an 8% increase on this year's per-unit rate, fixed for a two-year term" / Mr. Mostafa: "Good doing business with you again this year." | A new two-year term starting after one year | **Conflicts with 1.5** (a second two-year deal one year into the first). |
| L4 Capstone, rubric | 857 | "Earlier steps' history is genuinely used later on (the vendor relationship's first year …)" | One year of history | Consistent with "a year later", so it depends on the decision. |
| L4 Level Assessment, Part A sample | 846 | "If you can hold the increase at 4%, we can commit to a two-year renewal." | A different vendor (print and signage) | No conflict. Listed only so it isn't confused with the linen contract. |

**Passages affected by the choice:** L4 Lesson 1.3 (p. 629), Lesson 1.5 (pp. 640–642), Lesson 8.2 overlay (p. 819), Capstone Step 1 (pp. 848–849), Capstone rubric (p. 857).

**Options for the author** (choose one; none has been applied):

1. **The two-year contract is canonical.** Keep Module 1 as is and change the later passages. For example, the capstone becomes a call **two years later** when the 2027–2028 contract expires. Alternatively, Mr. Mostafa asks to reopen the rate early, and Arif holds him to the fixed term.
   - The 8.2 overlay changes in the same way: "returns at the end of the two-year term…".
   - This affects about 6 sentences in the capstone, plus 1 in 8.2.
2. **Annual renewal is canonical.** Change Module 1 so the deal is a one-year contract, and keep the capstone and 8.2 as they are.
   - This affects the core of Lessons 1.3–1.5: the conditional trade "two-year term" and the "fixed for the full two years" demand are the teaching example.
   - It is the larger change, and it alters the lesson's model negotiation.
3. **Another resolution** that the author specifies.

**Author's decision:** Option A, two-year contract (3 October 2026). Applied.

## Decision C — Arif's Level 4 kiosk pilot is not mentioned in Level 5 Lessons 6.3–6.4

**What happens.** In Level 5, Arif proposes moving the front desk to self-check-in for most guests. Ms. Noor (6.3) and then Mr. Kabir (6.4) challenge him with a 2019 attempt "where half our guests refused to use it". Arif answers with data from "the two hotels in our group". He never mentions his own six-week kiosk pilot at this hotel in Level 4: a clear success that both challengers saw presented, and that Mr. Kabir questioned and accepted.

| Level 4 (what both challengers already know) | PDF page | Level 5 Lessons 6.3–6.4 (the challenge and Arif's answer) | PDF page |
|---|---|---|---|
| L4 5.1: "Arif has spent six weeks piloting a digital self-check-in kiosk at the front desk, and the results are good: average check-in time is down sharply, and guest complaints about queuing have nearly disappeared." | 728 | L5 6.2 (the vision Arif is presenting): "I want us piloting self-check-in for returning guests by next quarter, building on the lobby kiosk we already have." | 979 |
| L4 5.1: "The bottom line is: the check-in kiosk pilot has been a clear success, and I'm recommending we roll it out to all three floors." (Dr. Rahman and **Mr. Kabir** in the room) | 729 | L5 6.3, **Ms. Noor**: "I like the direction, but I was in this room in 2019 when we tried something similar, and half our guests refused to use it. | L4 5.2, Arif to **Mr. Kabir**: "even with both of those costs factored in, the pilot still comes out twelve percent cheaper per month…" / Mr. Kabir: "If the number holds up once I've seen it, I don't have an objection." | 735–736 | L5 6.3, Arif: "the 2019 pilot didn't fail because guests rejected the idea. Arif gives the same answer, citing the two other hotels. | 991 |
| L4 Capstone, Step 3: "With the kiosk software long since configured…" | 850 | L5 Capstone, Step 4: "the mobile check-in pilot (the first step in the self-check-in plan Arif presented in Module 6)" | 1061 |

**Assessment.** This looks more like **(2) a continuity omission** than intentional characterization:

- Both challengers were present for, or approved, the Level 4 pilot.
- That pilot is the strongest and most local evidence Arif has.
- Level 5 6.2 itself mentions "the lobby kiosk we already have", so the text knows the kiosk exists.
- The lessons give no reason for Arif to set his own results aside.

It could still be read as **(1) intentional**: the Level 5 proposal covers "most guests" (a bigger change than one lobby kiosk), and the lesson wants evidence beyond a single property. Nothing in the text says so, however. It is not (3) another narrative issue, except that 6.4 repeats 6.3's objection and answer almost word for word with a different challenger; the lesson explains this as a full re-run of the presentation (p. 990).

**Options for the author** (none has been applied):

1. **Intentional; leave as is.** Optionally add one sentence in 6.3 explaining why Arif looks beyond the lobby pilot (e.g. the new plan is for most guests, not one kiosk).
2. **Continuity omission; add the Level 4 pilot to Arif's evidence** in 6.3 and 6.4 (and their answer keys, pp. 988–989 and 994). For example: "and our own lobby kiosk pilot cut check-in time by sixty percent once a staff member was beside it." The data move in PAT-0163 stays the same; only the evidence changes.
3. **Another resolution** that the author specifies, for example changing who raises the 2019 objection in 6.4.

**Author's decision:** Treatment A (3 October 2026). Applied.
